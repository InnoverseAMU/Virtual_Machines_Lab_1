# Treatment — Virtual Machines Lab

## 1. Three candidate structures

### A. The reveal ladder (chosen)
Start in the dark with one lit object — the 3D bench. Wires appear, power comes on, meters respond. Then pull back to the problem: in a real lab you don't wire, you only watch. The features fill the gap, one quick cut each, and the film ends on a single line: "Practise before you power on." The pacing is an acceleration from slow orbit to quick cuts, then a sudden stop.

### B. Before/after split
Split screen: left is a static lab manual page, right is the app. Each feature appears on the right as the left stays frozen. Ending superimposes the two. — Rejected: the split holds the frame hostage for 30 seconds and wastes half the screen.

### C. Countdown list
"Five things you can't do with a circuit diagram." Tick them off with features. — Rejected: the countdown structure forces equal time per item, and the opening is a text card, not the bench.

**Chosen: A — The reveal ladder.** It starts with the product, earns the problem, solves it with features, and ends on a tagline. The opening is a moving image, not text.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** A 3D electrical bench wires itself, lights up, and shows a student everything a diagram can't — before they touch 230 V.

- **Setup (0–8 s):** The 3D bench as a hero shot. Wires connect terminal to terminal, the supply switches on, meter needles sweep. The app is beautiful and alive.
- **Turn (8–16 s):** But the real lab is different. A circuit diagram beside a bare bench. Three short red warnings: 230 V, learning from a diagram only, one wrong connection can damage equipment. The gap between theory and practice is dangerous.
- **Resolution (16–27 s):** The features that fill the gap. Quick cuts: Learn mode explaining a step, a wiring fault caught with a red MCB TRIPPED banner, the observation table with a chart, the circuit diagram panel, Observe mode's pre-wired bench, the light/dark toggle and phone layout.
- **Ending (27–30 s):** One line: "Virtual Machines Lab. Practise before you power on." Credits: ZHCET, AMU, EEC2910, Asad Mokarim.

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn:**
- **Apple product reveals** (the dark stage, one object lit, slow orbit, snap transitions): how to make a software interface feel like a physical product launch.
- **Vercel/Linear launch films** (quick insert cuts for features, one accent, hairline grid, snap-grid precision): pacing under 60 s, feature density without clutter.

**Don't learn:**
- Their products, fonts, interfaces, icons, music, or any Apple/Vercel/Linear branding.

## 6. Cue map

**Tempo:** 120 BPM throughout (one beat = 0.5 s). Cuts land on beats or half-beats.

| Section | Bars | BPM | Instrumentation |
|---------|------|-----|-----------------|
| A: Bench hero (0–8 s) | 1–16 | 120 | Vibraphone arpeggio (single note, adding one per bar), low sine pulse on downbeats, shaker 8ths |
| B: Problem (8–16 s) | 17–32 | 120 | Strip to sine pulse only. Three tension stings (detuned marimba + filtered noise). Near-silence at bar 31–32 |
| C: Features (16–27 s) | 33–54 | 120 | Full palette: vibraphone chords, felt piano melody, glockenspiel accents on cuts, shaker. Each feature cut on a downbeat |
| D: End card (27–30 s) | 55–60 | 120 | Resolve: one sustained vibraphone chord, sine pulse fades, room tone |

## 8. Subtitle and title design

- **Captions**: Dark Tech Keynote style — a bottom-centre translucent pill with 1 px light border, Inter 500 at 40 px, a cyan accent dot. Rise + fade in 0.16 s.
- **Title text** ("Virtual Machines Lab"): Inter Tight 600, large, one line, with a light sweep reveal. Cyan accent glow.
- **End card credits**: Inter 400, smaller, three greys. Course code in JetBrains Mono, label style.
- Callout cards (problem section): dark surface cards with 1 px red border, Inter 500, red text. Pop-in with back easing.

**Accent colour: cyan `#00F2FE`** — matches the app's own dark-theme accent, which is already cyan/teal. This keeps the film true to the product.
